# Data Story Patterns

Use this reference when you already understand the source material and need to choose a Studio-friendly visual pattern.

Always prefer the current engine's existing cues and archetypes over inventing new ones.

## Quick mapping

### One claim + compact proof

Prefer:

- `hero-rail`
- `priority-rail`

Use when:

- one thesis should dominate the page
- support can stay in one smaller rail
- the page should feel editorial or product-led instead of dashboard-like

### Ranked priorities or ordered moves

Prefer:

- `priority-rail`
- `timeline`

Use when:

- you need clear ordering
- one item should visibly lead
- the page should guide action or sequence

### Two-sided verdict or tradeoff

Prefer:

- `verdict-comparison`
- `comparison`

Use when:

- the page compares two positions, options, or outcomes
- one side should visibly resolve as the winner
- the insight depends on contrast

### Numeric shift, trend, or relationship

Prefer:

- `chart-insight`
- `chart`

Use when:

- one trend or relationship drives the page
- one chart can do most of the visual work
- you only need sparse annotation around the main figure

### Research or technical method explanation

Prefer:

- `research-figure-stage`
- `figure`
- `center-stage-figure`

Use when:

- the page is about method, topology, or structure
- one central figure should dominate the page
- labels and notes should stay compact

### Case progression or event progression

Prefer:

- `case-timeline`
- `timeline`

Use when:

- the story unfolds through milestones
- the page needs visible progression
- too many equal cards would weaken the read

### Positioning or categorical mapping

Prefer:

- `matrix`
- `quadrant`

Use when:

- meaning comes from relative position
- the page is about categorization, tradeoffs, or strategic placement
- the audience should scan relationships instead of paragraphs

## Archetype-aware prompt language

These pairings are usually effective:

- `hero-rail` + `one dominant hero claim with a smaller evidence rail`
- `priority-rail` + `one leading priority, then ordered support`
- `verdict-comparison` + `one clear verdict, not a neutral comparison wall`
- `chart-insight` + `one dominant chart with sparse callouts`
- `research-figure-stage` + `one publication-like figure with compact labels`
- `case-timeline` + `one timeline with two or three milestones`

## Avoid mismatches

Bad mappings:

- asking for `chart-insight` when the source has no real numeric relationship
- asking for `matrix` when there are no dimensions or relative positions
- asking for `research-figure-stage` for a pure product marketing hero
- asking for several equal-weight charts when the real story has one main shift

## Practical default

If uncertain, choose the pattern that gives the page:

- one dominant visual
- one clear reading path
- compact support copy

That is usually safer than choosing the most complex layout.
